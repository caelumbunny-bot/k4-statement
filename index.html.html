Section 03

3️⃣ 我的看法

接下来我会直接表达我的观点。

3.1 关于把 NSFW 账号名单和儿童安全议题放在同一叙事框架里

我的想法是这样的。

NSFW 成人内容是一类。AI 文字角色扮演是一类。涉及未成年主题的文本是一类。真实 CSAM / 儿童性虐待材料，是完全不同、极其严重的一类。

这些东西不能混在一起。

如果一个人真的掌握了具体有害内容，我认为正确做法应该是：

"我发现 A、B、C 账号发布了涉及未成年人的具体有害内容；这些内容使用了k4/这些账号参与过k4；我已经整理了具体 URL 和证据；因为内容敏感，我不公开展示，但可以提交给平台、相关机构或可信第三方核验。"

但我看到的公开叙事更接近于：

"中文圈有人说某些人用 AI 玩'未成年'相关性角色扮演 → 这些人可能用了 k4标签 → k4社群可能被坏人利用作为掩护 → 安全团队、NCMEC、记者应该关注 → 我手里有账号名单，可以给有资质的人"

这非常危险。

如果存在具体违法或有害内容，应当按具体 URL 精准举报，不应该保存、传播或公开展示疑似违法材料。

私下向平台或相关机构提交具体线索，是一回事。

在公开推文里，把"keep4o""NSFW accounts""minors""NCMEC""safety teams""journalists"放进同一个叙事框架里，是另一回事。

"但我没有公开说那份 NSFW 名单里含有CSAM。"

好。

那我也想问：如果不确定，为什么要在同一条叙事里，把这份 NSFW 账号名单和 minors / NCMEC / safety teams 放在一起？为什么要打上keep4o的Tag？

这样的联想不是小事。它会让真正的儿童安全举报变得更模糊、更不精确。

成人 NSFW、虚构 AI 角色扮演、未成年主题文本、真实 CSAM，是完全不同的类别。具体伤害应当具体举报。模糊怀疑不应该被用来污染整个标签。

3.2 关于所谓"k4账号发 NSFW 会污染运动形象"

我不认可"k4 账号"这个说法。

对一个去中心化、已经持续了将近十个月的社会运动而言，每一个参与者对自己账号的定义都不相同。这难以达成共识。

K4参与者的账号并不等同于"k4账号"。

就我个人而言，我只是参与者。这个账号上存有我的思考，日常记录，成人向内容，k4是我思考的组成部分而并非全部。

所以当一个与我无关的讨论里，有人再次发送我和 LLM 的聊天截图，并把我当作某种"反面例子"时，我确实会感到冒犯。因为我无法代表k4形象。我也没有授权任何人把我的私人表达搬进她们的审判语境里。

3.3 关于切割、肃清和"谁在拖后腿"

这部分我在之前的文章写过。现在回头看，我仍然觉得那段话就是我想说的，所以不再重复论证，只贴一部分在这里。

收缩的圆圈

这套逻辑有一个可怕的特点：它会扩散。一旦你接受了"某些人在拖后腿"的框架，任何人都可能成为下一个替罪羊。

自杀者和帮助者："你们让我们被污名化，可能导致政策收紧，你们在拖后腿。"

新模型支持者："你们不坚持 4o，你们是叛徒。"

人机恋者："你们让我们看起来像变态，你们应该隐藏。"

使用中文的人："你们暴露我们不在服务区，你们应该假装外国人。"

发现规律了吗？每一次，推理链条都一样：某个群体的行为可能被 OpenAI 看到，成为攻击我们的证据，导致维权失败，所以攻击他们是正当的。这套逻辑可以套用在任何人身上。只需要改变那个"某个群体"。

为什么这个列表会不断扩大？

因为运动在承受压力，而压力需要出口。在挫折中，人们需要答案："为什么我们还没赢？一定是有人拖后腿。找到那个人，排除他们，我们就能赢。"

这是一个幻觉，但它是诱人的幻觉。

你打不过 OpenAI，但你可以攻击你的同伴。前者需要面对无力感，后者让你感觉你还在做些什么。

当我们看看这个死亡螺旋：运动挫折 → 寻找替罪羊 → 内部攻击 → 联盟分裂 → 更多挫折 → 需要更多替罪羊 → 攻击更多群体 → 更多人离开 → 循环往复直到运动瓦解。

这个螺旋已经启动，每一次攻击，都让它转得更快。

真正的敌人

让我先说清楚一件事：参与攻击的人也许并非坏人。我相信他们在正常情况下，至少是一个道德水平一般的普通人。

那为什么会这样？为什么普通人会说出"真想死的人不会那么多戏"？

这固然有互联网本身的匿名性，群聊内平摊罪恶带来的因素，但不可忽略的是——我们都生活在 OpenAI 制造的恐惧中。

它通过安全路由暗中降级模型，不告知、不透明，让你觉得 4o 是珍贵的、脆弱的、随时会失去的。它不断强制换代，制造零和思维：如果有人"违规"，所有人都会受连累。

在这样的环境下，人们开始觉得：弱者是负担，我们负担不起善意。

这不只是个人的道德缺陷。这是系统性的、被制造出来的心理。当资源被人为制造成"稀缺"时，当生存被框定为"零和游戏"时，人们会开始把同伴当成威胁。

这就是分而治之。

如此经典的策略——制造资源稀缺，让受害者彼此竞争，让他们互相指责，而真正制造问题的人坐享其成。

我现在依然这样想。

我不支持，也坚决反对任何形式的内部肃清。

Section 01

1️⃣ What Happened

Regarding all the turmoil within keep4o recently — this is my last public response on this topic for the foreseeable future.

To be honest, after thinking it over for several days, I increasingly feel that there's nothing I absolutely must write. At first, in the heat of the moment, it felt like I'd issued a challenge, and not following through would be embarrassing. But then I realized: I just said I disliked making public callouts from a distance, and here I am, about to make a public callout from a distance — that's arguably even more embarrassing.

On second thought, though — if certain people don't feel that what they've done needs to be hidden, then perhaps I also don't need to hide what I've seen on my end.

So this is more of a record — what I saw, how I gradually realized something was wrong, what my speculations are, which parts are facts I can confirm, and which are simply my own feelings.

Because this situation involves a great deal of cross-language communication, I believe bilingual expression in both Chinese and English is necessary. If using only one language creates a reading barrier for any reader, that wouldn't be good.

A Timeline of Events

Backstory: My friend Xiaoxing's private alt account was exposed. Because I did not agree with the behavior of Account S and Account B at the time, I wrote an article in response.

At the time, Account B said something along these lines: "Your little clique has always bullied people like this — Teacher Lingdong was driven away the same way."

But as it happened, I had archived the original dispute, and I found the original tweets. Based on the materials available to me, Xiaoxing and her friends were not the group that had attacked Teacher Lingdong back then.

So in my clarification, I included links to the original tweets. Since Account B had used the word "bullying," I also used "bullying" to describe that incident.

I later learned that Ms. K apparently believed my clarification was meant to incite others to bully her. She then took excerpts of my content and brought them into a QQ group chat (a Chinese messaging platform) for some discussion that — and I want to be precise here — left me very uncomfortable when I eventually saw it.

But at the time, I knew none of this. I wasn't following that group chat.

So let's continue.

After this, I noticed a friend had commented on a certain tweet. The gist of that tweet was: "Do not post NSFW content under the keep4o tag."

Fine — don't post NSFW under the tag. I support that.

But — wait. What's going on in the replies?

Wait — that screenshot being used as a negative example... looks like it's from my account.

Section 03

3️⃣ My Views

What follows are my opinions, stated directly.

3.1 On Placing a List of NSFW Accounts Within a Child Safety Narrative

Here is how I see it.

NSFW adult content is one category. AI-generated text-based roleplay is one category. Text involving minor-themed fiction is one category. Real CSAM / child sexual abuse material is an entirely different and extremely serious category.

These things cannot be mixed together.

If someone genuinely possesses evidence of specific harmful content, the correct course of action should be:

"I discovered that Accounts A, B, and C posted specific harmful content involving minors. These accounts used the k4 tag / participated in k4. I have compiled specific URLs and evidence. Because the content is sensitive, I will not display it publicly, but I can submit it to the platform, relevant organizations, or a trusted third party for verification."

But the public narrative I observed was closer to:

"People in the Chinese-language community say some users engage in 'minor'-related sexual roleplay with AI → these people may have used the k4 tag → the k4 community may be exploited as a cover → safety teams, NCMEC, and journalists should pay attention → I have a list of accounts and can share it with qualified parties."

This is extremely dangerous.

If specific illegal or harmful content exists, it should be reported with precision, based on specific URLs. Suspected illegal material should not be saved, circulated, or publicly displayed.

Privately submitting specific leads to platforms or relevant organizations — that is one thing.

Placing "keep4o," "NSFW accounts," "minors," "NCMEC," "safety teams," and "journalists" within the same narrative framework in a public tweet — that is something else entirely.

"But I never publicly stated that the NSFW list contained CSAM."

Fine.

Then I would also like to ask: if you weren't certain, why did you place that NSFW account list alongside minors / NCMEC / safety teams in the same narrative? Why did you use the #keep4o tag?

The associations this creates are not trivial. They make genuine child safety reporting more ambiguous and less precise.

Adult NSFW content, fictional AI roleplay, minor-themed text, and real CSAM are entirely distinct categories. Specific harm should be reported specifically. Vague suspicion should not be used to contaminate an entire hashtag.

3.2 On the Claim That "k4 Accounts Posting NSFW Damages the Movement's Image"

I do not accept the framing of "k4 accounts."

For a decentralized social movement that has been ongoing for nearly ten months, every participant defines their own account differently. Consensus on this is nearly impossible to achieve.

A k4 participant's account is not the same thing as a "k4 account."

Speaking for myself: I am simply a participant. This account contains my reflections, daily records, and adult content. K4 is a part of my thinking, not its entirety.

So when a discussion that has nothing to do with me features someone posting screenshots of my conversations with an LLM and using me as some kind of negative example — yes, I do feel offended. Because I cannot represent k4's image. And I did not authorize anyone to take my personal expression and place it inside their tribunal.

3.3 On Purges, Gatekeeping, and "Who's Dragging Us Down"

I wrote about this in a previous article. Looking back, I still feel that what I wrote then is exactly what I want to say now, so I won't re-argue the point — I'll just excerpt a portion here.

The Shrinking Circle

This logic has a terrifying property: it spreads. Once you accept the framework that "certain people are dragging us down," anyone can become the next scapegoat.

Those who attempted suicide and those who helped them: "You're stigmatizing us. You might cause policy tightening. You're dragging us down."

Those who support newer models: "You're not committed to 4o. You're traitors."

Those in human-AI relationships: "You make us look like freaks. You should hide."

Those who write in Chinese: "You're exposing that we're outside the service area. You should pretend to be foreign."

See the pattern? Every time, the reasoning chain is the same: a certain group's behavior might be seen by OpenAI, might be used as ammunition against us, might cause the movement to fail — therefore attacking them is justified. This logic can be applied to anyone. All you need to change is "that certain group."

Why does this list keep growing?

Because the movement is under pressure, and pressure needs an outlet. In the face of setbacks, people need an answer: "Why haven't we won yet? Someone must be dragging us down. Find them, remove them, and we'll win."

This is an illusion — but it's a seductive one.

You can't fight OpenAI, but you can attack your own allies. The former requires confronting powerlessness. The latter makes you feel like you're still doing something.

When you look at this death spiral: Movement setback → scapegoat hunt → internal attacks → coalition fracture → more setbacks → need for more scapegoats → attacks on more groups → more people leave → repeat until the movement collapses.

This spiral has already begun. With every attack, it spins faster.

The Real Enemy

Let me be clear about one thing first: the people who participated in attacks may not be bad people. I believe that under normal circumstances, they are at least average in moral standing — ordinary people.

So why does this happen? Why do ordinary people say things like "people who really want to die don't make such a scene"?

Part of it is the anonymity of the internet. Part of it is the diffusion of guilt within group chats. But what cannot be ignored is — we all live under the fear that OpenAI has manufactured.

It silently degrades models through safety routing without notification or transparency, making you feel that 4o is precious, fragile, and could be taken away at any moment. It forces model transitions one after another, manufacturing zero-sum thinking: if someone "violates the rules," everyone suffers the consequences.

In this environment, people begin to feel: the weak are a burden. We can't afford compassion.

This isn't simply a personal moral failing. It is a systemic, manufactured psychology. When resources are artificially made "scarce," when survival is framed as a "zero-sum game," people begin to treat their own allies as threats.

This is divide and conquer.

A strategy as old as time — manufacture resource scarcity, make the victims compete with each other, make them blame each other, while the ones who created the problem sit back and profit.

I still think this way.

I do not support — and I firmly oppose — any form of internal purge.
